NISSA Inlet

Nested Inlet for Sampling Stratospheric Aerosol

ANSYS Fluent · RANS k-ω SST and k-ε · Parametric design · NASA WB-57

Sampling aerosol from a fast aircraft requires slowing the air inside the inlet. This deceleration can generate turbulence, which removes particles from the sample. NISSA uses a nested design to prevent this.

  • Outer section: shrouds the sample so the flow is unaffected by the aircraft's angle of attack.
  • Middle section: samples isokinetically and slows the flow without adding turbulence.
  • Final section: sub-samples the slowed flow and turns it into the aircraft plumbing.

The geometry resulted from a parametric CFD study in 2D and 3D with near-wall resolution y⁺ ≤ 1 and the k-ω SST and k-ε turbulence models. The inlet was then manufactured and flown on the WB-57.

Wind Tunnel and Flight Measurements

Checking the inlet flow against the model

Hot-wire anemometry · Pressure sensing · Wind-tunnel testing · MATLAB

Computational results are only useful if the real inlet behaves as the model predicts. NISSA flew seven research flights on the WB-57 during the SABRE 2025 test campaign. It carried a miniature hot-wire, absolute and differential pressure taps and a DPOPS instrument. The DPOPS size distributions were compared with a balloon sounding of the same air mass.

  • In-flight turbulence intensity at steady cruise was about 19%. A ground wind-tunnel test on the manufactured inlet reproduced it (17 to 23%) with the probe at the flight station. The turbulence is therefore a real flow feature and not an instrument artifact.
  • The flight data reduction was built in MATLAB. The Teensy 4.1 flight electronics were calibrated against a laboratory reference chain.
  • Flight sampling at 5 Hz preserves mean velocity and turbulence intensity. It does not support spectra.

Detailed flight results are in preparation for publication. Flight plots are therefore not shown here.

Time period
2025 flights and 2026 ground tests
Collaborators
Prof. Suresh Dhaniyala, NOAA and NASA flight teams

Background
Ground tests give a controlled check on what the flight sensors measured.

Flight Control and Data Box

Teensy 4.1 · LattePanda · Hot-wire mini-CTA · Data acquisition

For the WB-57 test flights I built the electronics that ran the hot-wire and pressure measurements. The box must be reliable and safe to fly. Its measurements must stay accurate at altitude.

  • Teensy 4.1: microcontroller that reads the sensors and logs to storage.
  • LattePanda: single-board computer with a USB hub for control and data handling.
  • ADC: digitizes the analog signals, including the hot-wire mini-CTA output.
  • Power: conditioning boards, a solid-state relay and fuses to run the box from aircraft power.

The box was calibrated against a laboratory reference chain. A MATLAB pipeline converts its raw logs to velocity and turbulence intensity.

Time period
2025, for the SABRE flights
Collaborators
Prof. Suresh Dhaniyala, NOAA and NASA flight teams
My role
Designed and built the box. Calibrated it. Wrote the data pipeline.
Background
Compact flight electronics for hot-wire and pressure measurements inside the inlet.

LiDust Emission Monitor

Optical design · Prototyping · GD&T · Field testing

As a Project Associate in the EnERG Lab at IIT Madras (2019 to 2021) I led the design, build and testing of two prototypes of an optical dust-emission monitor. The work ran from concept to finished hardware with a team of engineers and technicians. I designed the optical system, a laser with a beam splitter and detector. The label on the prototype states the idea: when other sensors lie, trust an independent optical measurement.

  • Production drawings to ASME GD&T practice in Autodesk Inventor.
  • Field test of the prototype at India's largest thermal power plant (NTPC Vindhyachal).
  • Isokinetic gravimetric sampling data, analyzed in MATLAB and LabVIEW, served as the reference for the optical method.
Time period
2019 to 2021
Collaborators
EnERG Lab engineers and technicians, IIT Madras
My role
Led design, build and testing of two prototypes. Designed the optical system.
Background
Optical monitoring of dust emissions from industrial stacks.

PurpleAir Sensor

Aspiration efficiency of the PA-II-Flex

ANSYS Fluent · Discrete random walk · Low-cost sensors

Low-cost sensors such as the Plantower PMS5003 report PM1, PM2.5 and PM10. The housing around the sensor determines which particles reach it. The PurpleAir PA-II-Flex enclosure was modeled in 3D to compute the fraction of particles of each size entering the sensor at five wind speeds (0.4, 3, 5, 10 and 20 m/s), with and without turbulent dispersion.

  • 3D model of the housing and sensor slit with k-ε and k-ω SST turbulence models and Fluent discrete random walk particle tracking.
  • Results were compared with classical sampling correlations. These correlations do not capture the flow entering this enclosure.
  • The work was presented at AAAR. Field-data validation is the next step.
Time period
2022 to 2023
Collaborators
Prof. Suresh Dhaniyala
My role
Built the 3D model. Ran the CFD and particle-tracking simulations. Compared them with published correlations.
